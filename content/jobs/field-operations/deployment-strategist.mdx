# Deployment Strategist

### The Mission

Technology that clients do not adopt is technology that does not matter. The Deployment Strategist is the operational translator between Chemist3's engineering capability and the messy, political, and deeply human reality of enterprise adoption. You map client problem spaces to technical solutions, drive operational adoption across skeptical organizations, and ensure that every deployment Chemist3 executes creates lasting, measurable impact — not shelfware.

This role exists because even perfect software fails without an adoption strategy. You will sit between client stakeholders and Chemist3 engineering teams, translating business requirements into technical execution plans, identifying workflow integration points, managing deployment sequencing, and coaching client operators through the behavioral changes that new tooling demands. You are not a project manager with a Gantt chart. You are a field operator who understands both the technology and the organizational dynamics required to make it stick.

If you have spent your career in a role where success was measured by slide decks delivered rather than systems adopted, this position will expose you. If you have driven real operational change inside complex organizations — and can prove it — you will find this role deeply rewarding.

### Core Responsibilities

- Conduct structured client discovery to map organizational workflows, decision hierarchies, technical environments, and adoption barriers before deployment planning begins.
- Translate client problem spaces into scoped technical requirements that Chemist3 engineering teams can execute against — ensuring alignment between what the client needs and what the platform can deliver.
- Design deployment sequencing plans that phase capability delivery against client operational readiness, training capacity, and change management timelines.
- Serve as the primary operational interface between client leadership and Chemist3 field engineering teams — managing expectations, surfacing risks, and maintaining alignment throughout engagement lifecycles.
- Drive operational adoption by embedding with client teams during rollout phases — conducting hands-on training, building usage playbooks, and identifying and resolving adoption friction in real time.
- Monitor post-deployment usage metrics and operational outcomes to validate that deployed systems are delivering the intended impact — and course-correct when they are not.
- Synthesize field intelligence from deployments into structured feedback for Chemist3 product and engineering teams — feature requests, usability issues, and competitive positioning insights.
- Manage engagement documentation: statements of work, deployment milestones, acceptance criteria, and client communication records.

### What We Look For

**Technical / Domain Competencies:**
- Sufficient technical literacy to read architecture diagrams, understand API integration patterns, and have substantive conversations with software engineers about system design tradeoffs.
- Experience with enterprise software deployment lifecycles — requirement gathering, scoping, phased rollout, training, and post-deployment support.
- Familiarity with data-centric platforms, analytics tooling, or infrastructure systems — enough to credibly translate between engineering teams and non-technical stakeholders.
- Strong structured communication skills — written proposals, deployment plans, executive briefings, and technical requirement documents.
- Experience in management consulting, solutions engineering, technical account management, or defense/intelligence program management.

**Mindset & Attributes:**
- Ability to operate in ambiguous, high-stakes client environments where requirements shift and organizational politics are real constraints.
- Direct communication style — you surface problems early and propose solutions rather than managing perceptions.
- Deep empathy for client operators — you understand that adoption is a behavioral change, not a software installation.
- Comfort with travel and extended on-site engagements at client facilities.
- Ownership mentality — if a deployment fails to achieve adoption, you treat it as your failure, not the client's.

### Operating Principles

- **Outcomes Over Deliverables:** A deployment plan that results in active, daily usage by client operators is success. A plan that results in a signed acceptance document but no adoption is failure.
- **Zero Layering:** You talk directly to the engineers building the system and the operators using it. There are no intermediary layers between you and reality.
- **Field-First Engineering:** Strategy is only as good as its contact with the operating environment. Your plans are built from field observations, not conference room assumptions.
