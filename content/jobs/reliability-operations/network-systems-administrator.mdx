# Network & Systems Administrator

### The Mission

Chemist3 Labs operates physical infrastructure — lab environments, development clusters, bare-metal servers, and networking equipment that form the foundation of our engineering and deployment operations. The Network & Systems Administrator is the operator who keeps this foundation running: provisioning hardware, managing network topologies, maintaining host operating systems, and ensuring that the physical and virtual infrastructure behind Chemist3's engineering work is reliable, secure, and performant.

This is not a help desk role. You will design and operate internal network architectures, provision bare-metal servers for development and testing clusters, manage hypervisors and virtual machine fleets, administer DNS and DHCP services, and maintain the physical lab environments where Chemist3 engineers build and test the systems that get deployed to clients. When the network goes down, when a disk fails, when a developer cannot reach a test environment — you are the person who fixes it.

If you have only administered cloud instances and have never racked a switch, terminated a cable, or debugged an ARP table, this role will be a steep learning curve. If you have maintained physical infrastructure in lab, data center, or field environments and can troubleshoot across every layer from Layer 1 to Layer 7, you will be an essential operator at Chemist3.

### Core Responsibilities

- Design, implement, and maintain internal network infrastructure: switching, routing, VLANs, firewall rules, VPN gateways, DNS, DHCP, and network access control for Chemist3 lab and office environments.
- Provision and maintain bare-metal servers: hardware installation, BIOS/firmware configuration, RAID setup, OS deployment, and lifecycle management including warranty tracking and hardware refresh cycles.
- Administer host operating systems (Linux, Windows Server) across development, testing, and internal production environments — including patch management, user access control, and system hardening.
- Manage virtualization infrastructure (Proxmox, VMware ESXi, KVM) and provide virtual machine provisioning, snapshot management, and resource allocation for engineering teams.
- Operate and maintain internal lab environments: isolated test networks, hardware test benches, staging environments for pre-deployment validation, and physical security controls for sensitive equipment.
- Implement and maintain backup systems: automated backup schedules, off-site replication, disaster recovery procedures, and periodic restore testing.
- Monitor infrastructure health: network utilization, system resource consumption, disk health, environmental sensors (temperature, power), and alerting for hardware failures or capacity thresholds.
- Maintain physical infrastructure documentation: network diagrams, rack elevations, cable management records, IP address management (IPAM), and asset inventories.

### What We Look For

**Technical / Domain Competencies:**
- Strong networking fundamentals: TCP/IP, OSPF/BGP basics, 802.1Q VLANs, spanning tree, ACLs, NAT, and experience with managed switches and firewalls (Cisco, Juniper, MikroTik, pfSense, or equivalent).
- Deep Linux systems administration: systemd, package management, storage administration (LVM, ZFS, mdadm), kernel parameters, and performance tuning.
- Experience with server hardware: Dell, HPE, or Supermicro — including out-of-band management (iDRAC, iLO), firmware updates, and hardware diagnostics.
- Virtualization platform administration: Proxmox, ESXi, or KVM — including storage backends, network bridging, and live migration.
- Familiarity with configuration management (Ansible, Puppet, Salt) and basic scripting (Bash, Python) for automation of routine administrative tasks.
- Understanding of physical infrastructure: UPS systems, PDUs, structured cabling, rack planning, and environmental monitoring.
- Network security fundamentals: firewall rule design, IDS/IPS, certificate management, 802.1X, and VPN configuration (WireGuard, IPsec).

**Mindset & Attributes:**
- Hands-on operator mentality — you are comfortable with a console cable and a rack as you are with a terminal and SSH.
- Methodical documentation habits — you maintain accurate records because you know that undocumented infrastructure is unmanageable infrastructure.
- On-call reliability — you respond to infrastructure incidents with urgency and systematic troubleshooting, not panic.
- Security-conscious defaults — you harden systems, restrict access, and segment networks because it is the right thing to do, not because an audit requires it.
- Proactive maintenance orientation — you identify and address hardware aging, capacity trends, and configuration drift before they become incidents.

### Operating Principles

- **Extreme Ownership:** If the network is down, it is your problem. If a server fails, it is your problem. Infrastructure reliability is a direct reflection of your operational discipline.
- **Zero Layering:** You work directly with the engineers who depend on the infrastructure you maintain. Feedback loops between infrastructure issues and engineering impact are immediate and unfiltered.
- **Outcomes Over Deliverables:** Infrastructure that is reliable, performant, and well-documented matters more than infrastructure that uses the latest technology but is fragile and opaque.
