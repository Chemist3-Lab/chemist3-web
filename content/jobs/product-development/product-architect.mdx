# Product Architect

### The Mission

Chemist3 Labs does not build isolated tools — we build platforms. The Product Architect is the integrative mind that holds the entire product surface in focus: translating business requirements from clients and leadership into technical roadmaps, ensuring architectural coherence across subsystems, and preventing the organizational entropy that turns platforms into collections of disconnected features.

This role exists at the critical intersection of engineering capability and business reality. You will define what we build, in what order, and why — grounding every decision in customer pain, technical feasibility, and strategic positioning. You are not a project manager tracking deliverables. You are not a product manager writing user stories. You are an architect who understands both the code and the customer well enough to make the hard prioritization decisions that determine whether Chemist3 builds a cohesive platform or a pile of bespoke integrations.

If you have spent your career writing PRDs that engineers ignore, this role will force you to confront why. If you have the technical depth to earn engineering trust and the strategic clarity to align business stakeholders, you will shape the trajectory of every product Chemist3 ships.

### Core Responsibilities

- Translate enterprise client requirements, market intelligence, and internal engineering insights into coherent technical product roadmaps with clear prioritization rationale and sequencing logic.
- Define and maintain platform architecture at the product level — ensuring that new features, services, and integrations compose into a coherent system rather than fragmenting into isolated tools.
- Own the product specification lifecycle: from problem definition and solution framing through technical design review, implementation oversight, and post-release validation.
- Conduct competitive and market analysis to identify capability gaps, positioning opportunities, and emerging client needs that should inform product investment decisions.
- Facilitate architectural decision-making across engineering teams — mediating tradeoffs between short-term client needs and long-term platform scalability, and documenting decisions in Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).
- Define and track product-level success metrics: adoption rates, deployment velocity, client expansion signals, and platform reliability indicators that measure real product impact.
- Collaborate with Deployment Strategists and Forward Deployed teams to incorporate field intelligence — client feedback, deployment friction, and competitive encounters — into product planning cycles.
- Manage cross-functional alignment between engineering, design, security, and business operations to ensure that product decisions account for implementation complexity, security requirements, and go-to-market constraints.

### What We Look For

**Technical / Domain Competencies:**
- Sufficient engineering depth to evaluate architecture proposals, understand system design tradeoffs, and have substantive technical conversations with senior engineers — ideally with a software engineering background.
- Experience defining product strategy for technical platforms: developer tools, data infrastructure, enterprise SaaS, or defense/intelligence systems.
- Strong analytical frameworks for prioritization: impact/effort analysis, RICE scoring, opportunity cost assessment, and strategic alignment evaluation.
- Familiarity with modern software architecture patterns: microservices, event-driven systems, API-first design, and platform extensibility models.
- Experience with product analytics, user telemetry, and data-informed decision-making without falling into the trap of optimizing metrics that do not correlate with customer value.
- Excellent written communication — product specifications, architecture documents, and strategic memos that are clear, precise, and actionable.

**Mindset & Attributes:**
- Integrative thinking — you can hold multiple competing constraints (client needs, engineering capacity, market timing, security requirements) in mind simultaneously and find solutions that satisfy the binding constraints.
- Intellectual honesty about uncertainty — you make decisions with incomplete information, but you are explicit about assumptions and revisit them when new data arrives.
- Engineering credibility — you earn the trust of the engineering team through technical understanding, not organizational authority.
- Client empathy grounded in operational reality — you understand what clients need, not just what they say they want.
- Comfort with saying "no" — you understand that product discipline requires refusing good ideas to pursue great ones.

### Operating Principles

- **Outcomes Over Deliverables:** A roadmap that results in a cohesive platform with high client adoption is success. A roadmap that ships features on schedule but fragments the product is failure.
- **Zero Layering:** Product decisions are made in direct conversation with the engineers building the system and the clients using it. Second-hand requirements are a source of error, not efficiency.
- **Field-First Engineering:** Product roadmaps are informed by deployment reality. Features that perform well in demos but fail in client environments are architectural failures, not deployment problems.
