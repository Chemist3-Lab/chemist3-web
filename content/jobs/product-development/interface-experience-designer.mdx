# Interface & Experience Designer

### The Mission

Chemist3 Labs builds systems that handle complex, high-dimensional data for operators who cannot afford to misinterpret what they are looking at. The Interface & Experience Designer is the person who ensures that complexity is navigable, that critical information surfaces at the right moment, and that the humans operating our tools can make faster, more accurate decisions because of how the interface was designed — not in spite of it.

This is not consumer product design. You will not be optimizing click-through rates or engagement metrics. You will be designing operational interfaces for analysts, engineers, and field operators who use our tools under time pressure, cognitive load, and real-world consequences. A poorly designed data visualization can lead to a wrong conclusion. A confusing workflow can cost hours during an incident. The standard for design quality here is measured in operational outcomes, not aesthetic awards.

If you have designed dashboards that look good in a portfolio but have never been tested against real operator workflows, this role will recalibrate your understanding of design impact. If you have obsessed over information hierarchy, error state handling, and the cognitive cost of every additional click in a complex workflow, you already think the way we need you to think.

### Core Responsibilities

- Conduct user research with operators, analysts, and engineers across client and internal teams — contextual inquiry, task analysis, and workflow observation to ground design decisions in real operational behavior.
- Design interaction systems for complex data interfaces: search, filtering, drill-down, comparison views, temporal navigation, and multi-panel layouts that handle high-dimensional datasets without overwhelming the operator.
- Create wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity mockups for web applications, desktop tools, and client demonstration portals — iterating rapidly based on engineering feasibility and user testing feedback.
- Develop and maintain Chemist3's design system: component libraries, typography scales, color systems, spacing frameworks, iconography, and interaction patterns that ensure visual and behavioral consistency across all surfaces.
- Design for edge cases, error states, and degraded conditions — empty states, loading behaviors, timeout handling, permission boundaries, and offline/disconnected scenarios.
- Collaborate tightly with Web Systems Engineers and Software Engineers to ensure designs are implemented with fidelity and that interaction behaviors perform correctly across browsers, screen sizes, and input modalities.
- Establish and document accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1 AA minimum) across all interfaces, conducting accessibility audits and remediation as part of the standard design workflow.
- Present design rationale to engineering and leadership — articulating tradeoffs between visual complexity, implementation cost, and operator cognitive load.

### What We Look For

**Technical / Domain Competencies:**
- Expert proficiency in Figma (components, auto-layout, prototyping, design tokens) with a portfolio demonstrating complex, data-heavy interface design — not just marketing pages.
- Strong understanding of information architecture, data visualization principles (Tufte, Few), and interaction design patterns for analytical and operational tooling.
- Working knowledge of frontend implementation constraints: CSS layout models, responsive design breakpoints, animation performance, and component rendering behavior.
- Experience designing within or building design systems — including token architecture, component API design, documentation, and governance.
- Familiarity with usability testing methodologies: think-aloud protocols, task completion analysis, heuristic evaluation, and A/B testing for workflow optimization.
- Prior experience designing for defense, intelligence, healthcare, or other high-stakes operational domains is a strong signal.

**Mindset & Attributes:**
- Operator empathy — you design for the person using the tool under pressure, not for the screenshot in the portfolio.
- Comfort with complexity — you do not simplify interfaces by hiding information. You simplify by organizing it so the right information is available at the right moment.
- Strong written and verbal communication — you can articulate why a design decision matters in terms of operator performance, not just visual preference.
- Collaborative engineering orientation — you understand implementation tradeoffs and design within engineering constraints rather than designing fantasy interfaces.
- Relentless iteration — you treat every design as a hypothesis to be tested, not a deliverable to be defended.

### Operating Principles

- **Outcomes Over Deliverables:** A design is successful when operators perform better with it — faster decisions, fewer errors, higher confidence. Pixel-perfect mockups that degrade operator performance are failures.
- **Field-First Engineering:** Design decisions are validated against real operator workflows in real environments, not against personas in a design studio.
- **Zero Layering:** Designers work directly with the engineers implementing their designs and the operators using them. Feedback loops are measured in hours, not sprints.
